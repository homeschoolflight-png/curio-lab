The Sensory Budget

capacity planning // hack zone experiment 06

You usually can't control that a demanding day is coming, or how intense the person or situation is. What you can control is what goes around it. This one's about managing the day before it happens, not fixing a spike after it's already hit.

01 — WHAT YOU CAN AND CAN'T CONTROL

Capacity isn't infinite, but it's also not a fixed number you're stuck with for the day. It's a running balance — demands draw it down, recovery fills it back up, and what matters most is the order things happen in, not just the total.

Some slots in the day below are locked — those are the things genuinely outside your control (someone else's needs, a scheduled commitment, an unavoidable interruption). A few are marked 🔓 your call — that's where the actual leverage is. Build the day and see where it lands.

03 — THE SCIENCE OF WHAT'S ACTUALLY CONTROLLABLE

Feeling a sense of control over a stressor — even a small amount, even just over its timing — measurably reduces how stressful it registers as, independent of the stressor's actual intensity (this goes back to classic control research like Rodin & Langer). That's not a mindset trick; it changes the physiological response.

This is also the difference between reactive coping (dealing with a spike after it hits — which is what the Reactivity Dial simulator is for) and proactive coping — arranging things ahead of time so fewer spikes happen, and the ones that do land on a fuller tank. Proactive coping doesn't remove the demanding person or situation. It just makes sure you're not meeting it already empty.

Notice: even a full recovery later in the day doesn't undo a deep dip earlier. If capacity drops far enough, real things can happen at that moment — snapping, shutting down, saying something you don't mean — regardless of how the day averages out. The lowest point matters as much as the ending number.
