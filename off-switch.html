Your body runs two opposite modes: sympathetic (gas pedal — alert, fast, ready to react) and parasympathetic (brake pedal — rest, digest, repair). The main wire the brake pedal uses is called the vagus nerve. Flip the switch below to see what changes.

03 — THE SHORT VERSION

The vagus nerve is the longest wire in the parasympathetic system. It runs from your brainstem down through your neck, chest, and abdomen, touching your heart, lungs, and gut along the way.

When it's active, it tells your heart to slow down, your gut to keep digesting, and your whole body that it's safe to stand down from alert mode. When it's quiet, the sympathetic system is running the show instead — faster heart rate, paused digestion, pupils wide.

The nerve has stretch receptors in your lungs and diaphragm. A slow inhale followed by a longer exhale physically pulls on those receptors — that's the actual mechanical trigger, not a metaphor. Try it below.
